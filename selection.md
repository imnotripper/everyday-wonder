# Selection — Everyday Wonder, Two Days to Find the Line

Judge criteria, in order: (1) catches a small moment of wonder in everyday
life in a single sentence; (2) turns the moment at an angle the reader has
not met before, so the phrasing itself is the surprise; (3) lands its
surprise in one read, staying a single clear sentence; (4) grounds the
surprise in something real and specific; (5) keeps every word working, no
filler, no worn phrase.

Note: the brief itself gives three examples (lift door on a stranger's song,
the pause before a kettle clicks, a puddle holding the whole sky). Those
frames are disqualified here — an angle the reader has already met in the
brief cannot win on criterion 2.

## Candidates (10, all ≤200 chars)

1. `The lift doors opened on a stranger humming my dead grandmother's favourite song, and for one floor I believed in ghosts.` — DISQUALIFIED (brief's lift/song example)
2. `My kettle clicked off a second before the water boiled, as if it had changed its mind about being useful.` — DISQUALIFIED (brief's kettle example)
3. `A puddle on my street held the whole sky, and for a moment the pavement was the only thing keeping me from falling up.` — DISQUALIFIED (brief's puddle example)
4. `The escalator carried me past a man reading my exact book, and we both looked up at the same wrong word.` — 107
5. `At 3 a.m. the fridge hummed a note I recognised from a song I have never heard.` — 81
6. `The bus stop shelter smelled of rain that hadn't fallen yet, and everyone waiting stood a little straighter.` — 109
7. `My shadow on the crossing arrived a half-step before I did, as if it knew the way better than me.` — 97
8. `The lift mirror showed me older than I am, and for a second I apologised to the man I will become.` — 100
9. `A pigeon on the wire tilted its head at my phone, and I lowered the volume out of politeness.` — 96
10. `The vending machine returned my coin with a sigh, and I realised I had been holding my breath for it.` — 104

## Elimination

- Round 1 (brief's own examples): cut 1, 2, 3 — the reader has met those angles in the brief.
- Round 2 (C2 unmet angle): cut 4, 9, 10 — mild turns or familiar devices.
- Round 3 (duel 7 vs 8 vs 5 vs 6): #7 wins. #8's mirror is a known
  trope; #5's unheard song is harder to ground; #6's turn is softer.
  #7's shadow arriving first is a single clear image with a sharp turn.

## Winner

**7** — `My shadow on the crossing arrived a half-step before I did, as if it knew the way better than me.` (97 chars)

Why it wins: it is a small everyday moment (C1: a crossing, a shadow);
the angle is unmet (C2: the shadow arrives first, knowing the way better
— the phrasing itself is the surprise); it lands in one read as a single
clear sentence (C3); it is grounded in something real and specific (C4:
the crossing, the half-step); and every word works (C5: "half-step" is the
measure, "better than me" is the sting). The disqualified #1 (grandmother's
song, ghosts) was the strongest emotional line, but it wears the brief's
own frame — the shadow is the honest winner.
